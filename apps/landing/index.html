When a protocol’s admin keys sign what it never declared, its treasury is paid.

Mandate is parametric cover for Solana protocols against unauthorized use of privileged access. Attestors compare each privileged transaction with what the protocol declared in advance; the vote that completes their quorum carries the payout. On devnet, with test capital — terms below.

Insurance covers bugs in code. The largest loss of 2026 carried valid signatures — the contract did exactly what it was told.

What the protocol wrote down

A declaration entry names an operation the protocol expects its own keys to perform:

program
which program it calls
operation
which instruction
window
from when to when — or permanent
moves funds
yes or no

A new entry is in force only after a delay — 30 seconds on this deployment — so a stolen key cannot declare its own theft and act on it at once.

Revoking or narrowing is immediate.

Permanent entries are allowed only for operations that move no funds and hand over no control — a pause, not a withdrawal.

Entries are written with mandate-declare and signed by the protocol’s own key, or emitted unsigned for its multisig.

What the key signed

Every instruction of a privileged transaction is checked against the entries in force at that second. One instruction without an entry is enough — the incident above carried a genuine maintenance approve and a transfer nobody declared.

The vote that pays

Three attestors watch; two must agree. The second vote is not followed by a payout — it is the payout, in the same instruction. There is no appeal window.

Terms of this deployment

Today
  • Devnet only. No mainnet, no external audit — both come before any real money.
  • All three attestors are run by us. The quorum is counted honestly; its independence is arithmetic, not real. No stake and no slashing yet.
  • The capital in every pool is ours, and so are the policies.
  • No real protocol is covered. The protocols are test protocols; the ten compromises are shapes, not any company’s incident.
  • USDC here is a devnet test token.
  • There is nothing to buy, deposit or connect.
Never
  • Cover for code exploits, oracle failures or de-pegs.
  • Blocking, co-signing or delaying a transaction — it watches and pays, after the fact.
  • Splitting the payout among the protocol’s users. It pays one treasury and stops.
  • Recovering stolen funds.
  • A human appeal against the quorum.

A fixed sum pays more than a small loss and less than a catastrophic one. That is the price of not assessing damage.
